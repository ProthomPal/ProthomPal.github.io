Hitting the 40% retention target, build by build

How four consecutive builds of My Cooking Story resolved every major level-design wall found along the way and carried Day-1 retention past its 40% target — one level, one funnel and one loading hang at a time.

This page covers analysis and design reasoning only. Build internals, raw event exports and unreleased tuning tables are left out. Confirm with Rendered Ideas before publishing anything here that isn't already public.

SECTION 01

The starting point

My Cooking Story is a tycoon-style cooking and time-management game: players build a restaurant across sequential themes, serving customers within a level's timer and target. The north-star metric was Day-1 retention at a 40% benchmark — the share of new players who come back the day after installing.

Across four builds I tracked level-by-level progression, funnel depth, session loading behaviour and retention side by side, using each build's data to find the next lever before moving on.

Every build answered one question and raised the next. That loop is the whole story.

SECTION 02

Resolving the level-design walls

Three separate difficulty spikes surfaced across the four builds, each at a different point in the early-to-mid game. Tracking the same levels across every build shows each one being found and resolved in turn.

SECTION 05

Design framework: reward pacing

Level design and reward cadence are tuned together, following a three-act structure across the game's level range. Onboarding levels reward every clear to build habit; the mid-game spaces rewards out to create breathing room between pushes; the late game shifts to bigger, rarer payouts that reward mastery rather than frequency.

Act I — Onboarding
Reward every level
L1 → L15/16
Act II — Breathing gaps
Milestone every 4–5 levels
L16 → L60
Act III — Mastery stretch
Bigger, rarer payouts
L61 → L95

Reward cadence by act — the reward-frequency pattern the level list follows.

This cadence is deliberately asymmetric: dense, predictable rewards while players are still learning the core loop, widening as mastery increases, and a switch to weight-over-frequency in the endgame where players are optimising rather than discovering. Upgrade unlocks and new-content introductions are kept off the same level as each other wherever possible, so no single level asks a player to absorb two new things at once.

SECTION 06

Design framework: currency sources & sinks

The soft-currency loop follows a standard tycoon-economy structure: a small number of high-volume sources feed a small number of high-value sinks, keeping the loop legible without over-simplifying player choice.

Share of soft currency earned and spent

Illustrative share of total currency earned (sources) and spent (sinks) across a typical playthrough.

On the source side, level-clear rewards are the backbone of the loop — a predictable, always-available baseline — supplemented by milestone chests at set intervals, a daily login bonus to anchor return visits, and an optional ad-watch bonus for players who want to accelerate.

On the sink side, station upgrades are the primary spend: they visibly and immediately improve throughput, which keeps the upgrade loop satisfying. Then come container upgrades, speed boosters for players in a hurry, and item unlocks that gate new recipes behind progression.

SECTION 07

Investigation: session loading time

Early in the investigation, a drop early in the onboarding funnel looked like a candidate for a load-time problem. The current build carries direct loading-time telemetry for dropped-off sessions, which let the theory be tested directly.

Load time for the 145 users who dropped off at a level-load moment

80% of these users loaded in under 6 seconds.

Across all 95 levels, per-level average load time showed essentially no correlation with that level's drop-off rate. The small number of longer sessions were isolated, not concentrated on any particular level or device pattern — confirming that load time was never the retention bottleneck it first appeared to be.

The data ruled out a systemic slowdown, confirming performance was never the retention bottleneck.

SECTION 08

Day-1 retention: target achieved

Every fix above has to answer one question: does it move the metric the team is actually held to? The current build is the second with retention instrumented, and the movement is the clearest result in this case study.

Day-1 retention across builds, cohort-weighted

v8 and v14 predate retention instrumentation. The prior build's figure uses its full live install window (4,171 installs) for the most reliable read; the current build reflects a 1,443-install cohort.

D1 retention moved from roughly 33.8% in the prior build to 44.7% in the current one — a +10.9 point gain on a cohort large enough to trust, clearing the 40% target with room to spare.

SECTION 09

What this case study shows

01 — Difficulty spikes need to be tracked, not just fixed once.

Three separate spikes surfaced across four builds, each in a different part of the early-mid game. Re-checking the same levels build over build is what let each one be caught and resolved.

02 — Data can rule out a theory as usefully as it confirms one.

What looked like a loading-time issue in the onboarding funnel turned out to be a fast, well-performing session experience for the large majority of players — closing that line of investigation with confidence.

03 — Retention gains lagged level-design gains, then caught up.

D1 retention held flat through the build that resolved Level 11, and only jumped once the Level 15–16 fix landed and the funnel got meaningfully deeper. Not every improvement shows up immediately in the top-line metric.

04 — Compounding fixes cleared the target with room to spare.

A 40% target sounds like a narrow needle to thread until three of four markets clear it independently and the fourth sits within 1.3 points — evidence that the underlying game, not a lucky cohort, is what moved the number.

Outcome

My Cooking Story moved from an unstable early build to a validated, retention-positive live game. Every major level-design wall found in the process was resolved, and Day-1 retention now sits comfortably above its 40% target.
